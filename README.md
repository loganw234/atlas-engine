# atlas-engine

A language for platonography, grown from the plates rather than
legislated over them.

## The idea

Sixty-eight plates in PrettyCloud each hand-write the same deep
structure in GLSL: a function from a unit of randomness to a deposited
point of light, accumulated until the picture converges. Brightness =
density = measure is already the practice's motto, which means every
plate is secretly a program whose meaning is a measure, and the
renderer is its evaluator. This repository is where that stops being a
secret: a notation for stating subjects directly, and an engine that
evaluates the notation into the artifacts the practice already trusts.

## Terms

The practice reads photographically, so the language does too.

- A **positive** is a program in this language: the subject itself,
  stated. The term is the operator's, and it earns its keep twice
  over: the positive is what you finally see, and the process that
  realises it is depositive - the evaluator deposits points until the
  measure shows.
- **Exposure** is evaluation: running a positive under a sample
  budget.
- The **negative** keeps its existing meaning, untouched: the raw
  accumulation the darkroom stores from a render. Downstream of this
  engine, exactly as before.
- The chain reads: positive, exposure, negative, print.

## What the engine owes its neighbours

- **To PrettyCloud** (canonical plates): emitted GLSL that honours the
  registry contract - `shape_<id>(q, rnd, seed, P, col)`, eight
  levers, the cull sentinel - so an emitted plate is simply a plate.
- **To atlas-darkroom**: nothing new. The darkroom consumes plate GLSL
  verbatim and pins the exact program a print run uses; bit-identity
  remains its per-program guarantee and is not a constraint on this
  stage (operator's ruling, 2026-08-22). The engine's own bar is
  cell-level correctness, probed and measured.
- **To the operator**: the plates' verification bar, inherited whole:
  claims measured on pixels, structural predictions landing where the
  arithmetic says, and negative controls proving the instrument can
  see absence.

## Contents

Start here:

- `positives/` - the corpus. Sixty-eight plates plus `psf`, each a
  positive: real JavaScript that the CPU evaluator RUNS and the emitter
  READS. Read `hopf.pos.mjs` and `jong.pos.mjs` first; they are the
  register the rest aim at.
- `core/measure.mjs` - the evaluator. The stream, the orbit, the
  descent, the deposit.
- `core/emit.mjs` - the emitter. The same source parsed into
  registry-contract GLSL, pinned, refusing what it cannot prove honest.
  It never writes an unpinned float operation - not in an expression,
  not in a call argument, and since 2026-08-23 not in a comparison
  either, which is the one place arithmetic decides control flow.
- `docs/CONVERSION.md` - how to write one, what the subset refuses, and
  the two loop shapes that keep a driver's unroller off a plate.
- `docs/TEMPLATE.pos.mjs` - an annotated positive to copy. It is a real
  one and is meant to stay one: if it stops emitting, the vocabulary
  moved under it.

And the rest:

- `tools/survey.mjs` - measures the construct vocabulary of the whole
  plate corpus (reads the darkroom's `plates.json` dump; the survey
  names the corpus commit it measured).
- `docs/SURVEY.md`, `docs/survey.json` - generated by the tool.
- `docs/VOCABULARY.md` - the analysis: what the survey says the
  language is, construct by construct, and the first milestone.
- `core/constants.json` - the pinned constants, each with its bit
  pattern, exact decimal, provenance, domain and measured bound.
- `tools/oracle.mjs` - the only way the engine may reach a constant.
  Re-decodes every bit pattern in JavaScript and refuses a record whose
  redundant fields disagree.
- `tools/verify-constants.py` - checks that record at three levels
  (provenance, transcription, behaviour) using mpmath at 50 digits,
  deliberately a different language and lineage from the code that
  consumes it. Plus a seal over the bit patterns and a cross-check
  against the darkroom generator the values were transcribed from.
- `tools/verify-negative-controls.py` - nine deliberate corruptions the
  checker must catch. A checker that passes is not evidence until you
  have watched it fail.
- `docs/DETERMINISM.md`, `docs/CONSTANTS-FINDINGS.md` - the plan, and
  what the oracle found.

## Status

**Sixty-eight of sixty-eight, on four columns.** Every plate in the
atlas has a positive; the engine emits all of them pinned; and the
bundle those emit into produces ONE HASH on an RTX 5060 Ti, a GTX 1080,
an RX 7600 and an Arc B580 - three vendors, four architectures, two
NVIDIA generations seven years apart and two independent Mesa drivers,
with no plate erroring on any column. Since 2026-08-23 that bundle is
what the darkroom renders photographs from, in place of the
hand-written GLSL it corrected afterwards for a year.

The record of how, including what it cost:
`atlas-darkroom/docs/test-records/2026-08-23b-…`.

What that number rests on, stated so it cannot be read as more than it
is: the `cpu` rung, 1024 square; these four columns and not "any card";
and llvmpipe excluded for a measured reason (it does not single-round
`fma()` even under `precise`, which is a fault in its own lowering and
puts no GPU at risk).

### Running the gates

```
node tools/verify-pinned.mjs      no unpinned op, no unbound comparison
node tools/compile-pinned.mjs     every positive emits, pinned and plain
node tools/ci-smoke.mjs           all 69 walks, against a named record
node tools/verify-orbit-block.mjs orbit block bodies
```

Those four are pure node and run in CI (`.github/workflows/gates.yml`),
whose last step prints what a green tick does NOT cover. The rest -
`tools/compile-pinned.py --run`, `tools/detbits.py`, the four CDP
probes, `tools/verify-constants.py` - need a GL context, a browser, a
GPU per column or the darkroom beside this checkout, and are named
there rather than left as a silence.

### Three of the sixty-nine fail one smoke row

`buddha`, `qjulia` and `vlsi`, each at one hashed lever setting, with
the measurement and the rule it trips in
`docs/known-smoke-failures.json`. They are real signals and none is a
determinism question. The list fails both ways: an unlisted plate that
starts failing stops the build, and a listed plate that starts passing
stops it too.
